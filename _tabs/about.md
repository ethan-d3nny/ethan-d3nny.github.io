I'm Ethan, a third-year cyber security student with a focus on reverse engineering, vulnerability research, and malware analysis. This blog is where I write up the technical projects I work on in my own time.

## What I work on

I spend most of my time taking things apart to understand how they work, and increasingly, how they break.

- **Malware analysis** — unpacking samples, writing automated configuration extractors, and building emulation-based scripts to recover obfuscated strings (such as custom stack-string decryption routines).
- **Vulnerability research** — reverse engineering Windows drivers with known vulnerabilities to understand root causes, and working through vulnerability research and exploit development challenges.
- **Protection mechanisms** — studying how commercial anti-cheat and integrity-checking systems (including VAC and Hyperion) detect tampering and resist analysis.
- **Low-level internals** — building tools from the ground up to understand how executables are loaded and run, including a manual PE mapper in C++ and a small virtual machine built to execute a disassembled instruction set.

I work primarily in **C/C++** and **Python**, with some **x86 assembly**, using tools including **IDA**, **Ghidra**, **Binary Ninja**, **x64dbg**, **GDB**, and **GEF**.

## Why I write this

Most of what I know came from pulling things apart until they made sense, and most of that work is invisible unless it's written up. This blog is where I document the process properly: not just the solution, but what I tried, what didn't work, and what I'd do differently next time.
